# Cap Torque Specification and Inspection Method

## Purpose and Scope

This specification defines the required application torque for screw closures on Riverbend Packaging bottles and the method used to verify it. Correct torque protects against leakers, tamper-band damage, and consumer complaints from caps that are too tight to open. It applies to all products run on the CX-4 capper.

## Torque Requirements

Application torque is measured as removal torque 24 hours after capping unless otherwise stated. The specification limits below apply to the measured value on a calibrated digital torque tester.

| Cap size | Bottle | Specification | Action limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 38 mm | 500 mL | **18 to 22 in-lb** | Below 17 or above 23 stops line |
| 28 mm | 250 mL | 12 to 16 in-lb | Below 11 or above 17 stops line |
| 33 mm | 1 L | 16 to 20 in-lb | Below 15 or above 21 stops line |

> **NOTE:** The specification applies to the finished package at the end of the line. Capper settings must be adjusted so that finished-package torque falls inside this range.

## Sampling Plan

| Production state | Sample size | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Startup or after changeover | 10 bottles | Before release |
| Normal production | 5 bottles | Every 30 minutes |
| After a jam or restart | 5 bottles | Immediately |
| Suspect lot | 20 bottles | As directed by Quality |

Sampled bottles are taken from all four capper heads, with at least one from each head, and labeled with head number.

## Inspection Method

1. Verify the torque tester has a current calibration sticker. Do not use an expired tester.
2. Zero the tester with no bottle installed.
3. Place the bottle in the fixture and secure the closure in the tester chuck.
4. Rotate the closure counterclockwise at a steady rate until the cap breaks free.
5. Record the peak value in the SPC log.
6. Repeat for the remaining samples.
7. Compute the average and range of the sample.
8. Compare the results to the specification and action limits.

## Disposition of Results

| Result | Action |
|---|---|
| All values within specification | Continue production |
| Any value within spec but average trending toward a limit | Notify maintenance for adjustment |
| One or more values outside spec but inside action limits | Resample 10 bottles; adjust capper |
| Any value outside action limits | Stop the line, notify Quality, hold product since last good check |

Product held for torque is handled under the Nonconformance and Hold Procedure. The Quality Engineer determines whether the product is reworked, sorted, or scrapped.

## Visual Inspection

In addition to torque, inspect for cocked caps, cracked tamper bands, scuffed threads, and missing liners. Reject any bottle with a cap not seated flush on the neck finish.

## Equipment and Calibration

Torque testers are calibrated annually by an external lab and verified monthly with a reference weight. Record the verification in the equipment log. A tester that fails verification is tagged out of service and all results since the last good verification are reviewed by the Quality Engineer.

## Relationship to Capper Settings

The capper clutch is a means to achieve the finished-package torque, not the requirement itself. Differences between clutch setting and finished torque occur because of liner relaxation and thread friction. Maintenance adjusts settings based on quality data, and Quality confirms the effect with the sampling plan above.

## Records

SPC charts and torque logs are retained for two years and reviewed weekly by the Quality Engineer. Out-of-specification events are reported at the weekly quality meeting.
