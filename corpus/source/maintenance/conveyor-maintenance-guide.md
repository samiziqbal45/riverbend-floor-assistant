# Conveyor Maintenance and Jam Troubleshooting Guide

## Overview

This guide covers routine maintenance and jam troubleshooting for conveyors C1 through C4 at Riverbend Packaging. The conveyors are chain-driven table-top lines with variable-frequency drives, guide rails, and transfer plates between sections. Keeping the lines clean and properly tensioned prevents most jams.

## Specifications

| Item | C1-C2 | C3 | C4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chain type | Plastic table-top | Plastic table-top | Roller chain |
| Drive | 1 hp VFD | 1 hp VFD | 2 hp VFD |
| Normal speed | 60 ft/min | 60 ft/min | 40 ft/min |
| Jog speed | 8 ft/min | 8 ft/min | 6 ft/min |

## Preventive Maintenance

| Interval | Task |
|---|---|
| Daily | Remove debris and glass; check guide rail alignment |
| Weekly | Lubricate chain with approved dry lube; check tracking |
| Monthly | Inspect sprockets, wear strips, and idlers |
| Quarterly | Check chain tension and drive coupling |
| Annually | Replace worn chain sections; inspect gearbox oil |

### Chain Tension

Correct tension allows the chain to lift about 0.5 inch at mid-span. Loose chain jumps sprockets and causes stalls; tight chain overloads bearings.

1. Lock out the conveyor.
2. Loosen the take-up bolts.
3. Adjust until sag is correct and equal on both sides.
4. Tighten bolts and rotate by hand to confirm free movement.

## Jam Troubleshooting

| Jam location | Common cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Filler infeed | Bottles tipped by worn guide rail | Realign or replace rail |
| Transfer plate | Gap too large or plate worn | Adjust gap to 1/8 inch |
| Capper infeed | Cap or bottle debris | Clear and clean chute |
| Outfeed to packer | Packer stall backs up line | Check packer first |
| Return loop | Broken glass or labels | Clean return belt |
| Mid-line stall | Seized idler or overload | Replace idler; check amp draw |

### Clearing Small Jams

Small jams, such as a single fallen bottle on a straight section, can be cleared quickly with the conveyor in jog mode at reduced speed. Use the jog button on the local station to move the chain slowly and use a jam hook to free the container. Release the jog button as soon as the bottle is free. This keeps downtime short and avoids a full line restart.

For larger jams involving several bottles, a jammed chain, or a jam at a transfer point, stop the conveyor and use the isolation points at the disconnect before reaching in.

### Diagnosing Repeated Jams

1. Note the jam location and time on the downtime log.
2. Check guide rails for rubbing marks on bottles.
3. Measure the transfer plate gap with feeler gauges.
4. Confirm the line speeds between sections are matched within 2 percent.
5. Inspect the chain for stretched links.

## Motor and Drive Issues

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| VFD overcurrent fault | Jam or tight chain | Clear jam; check tension |
| Conveyor slow | Incorrect VFD parameter | Reset speed parameter |
| Motor overheating | Blocked fan or overload | Clean fan; check load |
| Gearbox noise | Low oil or worn gears | Check oil; schedule rebuild |

## Replacing a Chain Section

1. Lock out the conveyor and release tension.
2. Locate the damaged section and remove the pin.
3. Install a new section of the same pitch and material.
4. Reinsert the pin and retaining clip.
5. Restore tension and run empty for ten minutes.

## Wear Parts

Keep stocked: wear strips, plastic chain sections, guide rail brackets, idler bearings, and dry lube. Replace wear strips when worn to 1/16 inch thickness.

## Records

Log all jam events with location, duration, and cause. Review weekly for trends. Repeated jams at one location trigger a maintenance work order.
