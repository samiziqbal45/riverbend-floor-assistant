# RP-200 Centrifugal Pump Manual

## Overview

The RP-200 is a single-stage centrifugal pump that transfers product from the blend tank to the filler bowl on the Riverbend Packaging filling line. It is driven by a 5 hp motor through a close-coupled shaft and uses a single mechanical seal with a flush port. This manual covers normal operation, troubleshooting, and the preventive maintenance (PM) schedule.

## Specifications

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Model | RP-200 |

| Discharge size | 2 in. tri-clamp |

## Operation

### Startup

1. Confirm the blend tank is above the low-level mark. The pump must never run dry.
2. Open the suction valve fully, then crack the discharge valve one quarter turn.
3. Check that the seal flush line is open and the sight glass shows flow.
4. Press START at the local panel and confirm rotation matches the arrow on the casing.
5. Open the discharge valve slowly while watching the discharge pressure gauge. Normal operating pressure is 18 to 22 psi.

### Shutdown

1. Close the discharge valve slowly.
2. Press STOP and wait for the shaft to stop.
3. Close the suction valve if the pump will be idle for more than one shift.
4. Flush the pump with potable water if product will not be restarted within two hours.

> **WARNING:** Never run the RP-200 against a closed discharge valve for more than 30 seconds. Heat build-up damages the seal and can flash product to steam.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Seal leak at the shaft | Worn seal faces or dry running | Replace mechanical seal; check flush flow |
| Leak at casing clamp | Damaged gasket | Replace tri-clamp gasket |
| Low discharge pressure | Air in suction line or worn impeller | Re-prime pump; inspect impeller clearance |
| Pump noise or rattling | Cavitation or bearing wear | Check suction level; inspect motor bearings |
| Motor trips on overload | Closed valve or rubbing impeller | Check valve positions; inspect impeller |
| Product foaming | Air leak at suction fitting | Tighten fittings; replace suction gasket |
| Excess vibration | Misalignment or impeller imbalance | Check coupling; balance impeller |

### Seal Leak

Sanitizer or product leaking at the shaft indicates seal wear. Small weeping of a few drops per minute can continue for a limited time, but a steady stream requires immediate shutdown. To replace the seal:

1. Stop the pump and perform lockout/tagout on disconnect MD-6 and close product valves PV-1 and PV-2.
2. Drain the casing into a container and rinse residue.
3. Remove the casing clamp and slide the casing off the impeller.
4. Remove the impeller nut and impeller. Pull the old seal assembly from the shaft.
5. Clean the shaft and seal seat. Inspect for scoring.
6. Lubricate the new seal elastomers with food-grade lubricant only. Never touch the seal faces with bare fingers.
7. Reassemble in reverse order and torque the impeller nut to 35 ft-lb.
8. Remove locks and restore service. Run for five minutes and check for leaks.

Any chemical released from the seal must be handled under the Chemical Spill and Sanitizer Handling procedure, and product exposed to the leak must be reported to Quality.

## Preventive Maintenance Schedule

| Interval | Task | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | Visual check for leaks, noise, and vibration | 5 min |
| Weekly | Check seal flush flow and pressure gauge accuracy | 10 min |
| Monthly | Inspect gaskets and tri-clamps; verify coupling guard | 20 min |
| Quarterly | Lubricate motor bearings (2 pumps of grease) | 15 min |
| Annually | Inspect impeller, replace mechanical seal, megger motor | 3 hr |

## Spare Parts

Keep on hand: mechanical seal kit RP200-SK, tri-clamp gaskets (EPDM), impeller nut, and motor bearing set. Order lead time for the impeller is three weeks.

## Records

Record each PM task in the maintenance log with date, technician, and findings. Any seal replacement is noted with the run hours since the last replacement to support life-cycle tracking.
